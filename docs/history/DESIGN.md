# Cat Duet — Design Record (ultracode workflow wf_26b2c825-42b, 2026-09-24)

17 agents: 4 researchers, 4 designs, 8 adversarial refuters, 1 judge. 1.5M tokens, 224 tool calls, 45 min.

## Winner

Genjutsu Recast + Local Identity Lock (closest-to-example lens), re-scoped: submit only the medium/close segments, put the example's own closed-mouth cat in front of the user as a decision, and graft the now XPU-verified LivePortrait-animals path as an optional local lip pass on the cat.

## Why

Every local design was refuted on outcome quality by an adversary who actually looked at the footage, and the refutations hold: the RIGHT performer has a hand or the mic inside the head region in ~35 % of rapping samples and across his mouth in ~18 %, his arm crosses in front of the LEFT performer in the climactic last shot (visible at 25 s in the self-test sheet), the two men touch in most medium frames so a saliency matte cannot split them, there is a hidden reframe at 7.97 s plus a slow push-in in the main rapping shot, and the wide shots (24 % of runtime incl. the opening) show a full human body. No amount of tuning turns a cat head on an orange-polo, dreadlocked, ringed-hand body into the whole seated cat on a stool in flannel that the user pointed at; the honest local ceiling is 3-5/10 against the example after 20-45 h of build. Genjutsu is the tool that made the example, its endpoint was verified live (video_url 4-30 s, image_urls 1-8, 480p/720p, $0.318/$0.681 per second list, 480p discounted to $0.159/s until 2026-10-01), cuts are documented to stay in sync, and the local half (swap.py on DirectML) is already proven end-to-end. Two facts must be put to the user plainly before any money is spent: (1) in the user's own reference the cat's mouth is CLOSED in essentially every frame - Genjutsu sells 'rapping' with paws, lean and head bob, not lips - so 'mouth moving to the lines' is not what the example delivers; (2) the design's user-side identity lock is weak because the LEFT performer is at 57 deg median yaw in sunglasses, so Genjutsu's own reference-based identity does most of the work. The single biggest new fact from the verdicts is that LivePortrait animals v1.1 runs on the Arc 140T with real weights at 0.29 s/frame (torch 2.14.0+xpu, 1.2 GB), which makes a per-frame lip pass over Genjutsu's cat head a credible, cheap, local add-on if the user insists on visible lip motion - that is the graft that closes the one requirement Genjutsu does not meet.

## NPU verdict

The NPU is real but marginal here, and it is not on the critical path. Measured on this machine: buffalo_l detection (det_10g) runs 12-17 ms and the ArcFace embedding (w600k_r50) 6-7 ms on the Intel AI Boost NPU with decision-identical outputs, versus about 80 ms for detect+embed on DirectML - a saving of ~55-65 ms per frame, roughly one minute over an 872-frame pass. The heavy stages cannot move: inswapper_128 on the NPU is fast (124 ms) but FP16-only and visibly destroys the swap (20.4-23.6 dB PSNR), gfpgan is numerically fine but slower than the GPU (308-373 vs 260 ms), and LivePortrait's 5D GridSample is unsupported by OpenVINO/NPU entirely. In the recommended Genjutsu path the only local compute is one swap.py pass, so the NPU would save about a minute for 1-2 hours of work. Verdict: skip it for v1; if you later run the free LivePortrait lane, an openvino==2025.4.1 detection shim (never onnxruntime-openvino in the DirectML venv; 2026.x needs NPU driver 32.0.100.5540) is a small, safe way to free the Arc.

### Risks

- R1 - The example does not lip-sync the cat: the user's reference shows a closed cat mouth in every frame; Genjutsu will most likely return the same. If 'mouth moving to the lines' is a hard requirement, only the optional XPU LivePortrait lip pass (STEP 8, verified backbone but unverified paste-back on generative frames) or the free head-swap fallback delivers it, and both are damped (animal fine-tune dropped the lip loss; lip multiplier 1.9-2.3 needed). Decide at STEP 0.
- R2 - Genjutsu behaviour on a two-person scene with cuts, touching bodies and hand contact is undocumented; cat may land on the wrong side, both may merge, stool/flannel may drop in a segment. Mitigation: 480p probe first, block-only input, explicit LEFT/RIGHT prompt, baked glasses reference, retake the failing block only; never splice across takes.
- R3 - Cost creep and pricing uncertainty: API is $0.318/$0.681 per second list (480p discounted to $0.159/s until 2026-10-01); a 720p take of both blocks is ~$15, probe + 3-4 takes ~$25-60. Subscription tiers are third-party numbers only. Cap the budget before starting; use pay-per-second API if only a few takes are needed.
- R4 - User identity: the LEFT performer stands at 57 deg median yaw in sunglasses, and Genjutsu regenerates him full-body, so the inswapper lock only helps in frontal moments and conflicts with glasses-on-user. Rely on Genjutsu's reference identity first, gate the lock to |yaw| < 45 deg, and use it as a spot fix.
- R5 - Output geometry/fps unknown (example is 960x720 4:3 at 24 fps): finish.py must detect WxH/fps rather than assume; the 9:16 layout crops around the mic. Original audio is re-muxed regardless.
- R6 - Wide shots are dropped by design (intro and high-five): the final piece starts on the medium shot and is ~22 s, not 29 s. If the user wants the intro, it stays as untouched human footage, which breaks the illusion.
- R7 - swap.py as-is will sys.exit on a Genjutsu output (learn_performers needs two human faces, faces/B mean_embedding on an empty folder); the STEP 7 edits are mandatory before running it.
- R8 - Lip pass technical risk: per-frame source switching through SPADE can flicker at the paste mask, the cat head in Genjutsu output is ~150-250 px so the mouth is small, and the lip keypoint indices are inherited from the human pipeline (verify by perturbation). Gated by a 3 s test; shipping without it is acceptable and matches the example.
- R9 - Free fallback gaps are structural: cat head on the orange-polo body with hands, chains and lower dreads; hand occlusion in ~35 % of rapping samples needs the per-frame foreground matte or hands vanish into the cat; the main rapping shot has a hidden cut at 7.97 s and a slow push-in, so static plates are wrong there. Expect 4-5/10 against the example even after the fixes, 20-30 h of build.
- R10 - GPU/venv hygiene: torch XPU (.venv-lp) and ORT DirectML (.venv) both run on the Arc - sequential only, and only after the background self-test render finishes; never install onnxruntime-openvino or PyPI insightface into either venv; LivePortrait's requirements.txt pins onnxruntime-gpu (use the slim list).
- R11 - Rights/licensing: COLORS footage of real artists is fine for a private/portfolio meme, not for monetisation; LivePortrait animal weights carry research terms; insightface models are non-commercial.
